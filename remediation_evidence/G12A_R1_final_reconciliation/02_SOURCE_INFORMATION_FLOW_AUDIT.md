## 1. Information Set $\mathcal{I}_t$ Isolation & Data Flow Architecture

The core question of the G11/G12A causality audit is whether decision policy $\pi$ at epoch $t$ receives any information about future ground truth realizations ($> t$), particularly realized ground dwell and future gate clearance times.

### 1.1 Structural Boundary Analysis
The decision policy [`make_causal_recourse_decision()`](file:///D:/Study/Code/Python/Aelous/src/simulation/monte_carlo_engine_v2.py#L427-L515) operates strictly on the immutable snapshot [`CausalObservationSnapshot`](file:///D:/Study/Code/Python/Aelous/src/simulation/monte_carlo_engine_v2.py#L416-L425).

The data pipeline feeding $\pi(\mathcal{I}_t)$ enforces strict information isolation:

```
[Simulation World / Ground Truth Reality]
  ├── realized_flights: Sequence[Flight]
  │     ├── scheduled_arrival_min (constant pre-tactical)
  │     ├── scheduled_departure_min (constant pre-tactical)
  │     ├── predicted_arrival_min (= realized arrival t_arr)
  │     └── precomputed_gate_out_min (= realized future pushback t_dep)  <--- PROTECTED GROUND TRUTH
  │
  ▼ [Strict Causal Projection at Epoch t]
ObservableFlightSnapshot (Stripped)
  ├── flight_id, carrier, flight_number, aircraft_type
  ├── scheduled_arrival_min, scheduled_departure_min
  ├── observed_arrival_min = current_time (observable upon touchdown at t)
  ├── estimated_departure_min = max(sched_dep, t + min_turnaround, t + default_dwell)  <--- CAUSAL LOWER BOUND
  └── [precomputed_gate_out_min: DOES NOT EXIST]
  └── [realized_departure_delay: DOES NOT EXIST]
  └── [occupancy_end_min: DOES NOT EXIST]
```

### 1.2 Inspection of Precomputed and Derived Fields
A critical vulnerability identified in Task 1 is whether future information could enter the snapshot through precomputed fields:

1. **`precomputed_gate_out_min`**:
   - In `SimulationWorld`, this field represents the realized departure time generated by the Monte Carlo delay realization generator.
   - In `monte_carlo_engine_v2.py`, this field is accessed **only** by the simulation environment to determine whether an already-docked aircraft is still physically occupying the gate at epoch $t$ (`current_time < actual_vacate`).
   - If physically occupied, the vacate time communicated to the snapshot is **not** `actual_vacate`, but rather `causal_est_vacate = max(fl.scheduled_departure_min, fl.predicted_arrival_min + fl.min_turnaround_min, current_time) + fl.buffer_min`.
   - The decision policy `make_causal_recourse_decision()` never receives the `Flight` entity and cannot inspect `precomputed_gate_out_min`.
2. **`estimated_departure_min`**:
   - Computed exclusively from pre-tactical schedule and physical minimum turnaround constraints.
   - Mutating a flight's own realized departure delay in the simulation world does not alter `estimated_departure_min` at arrival epoch $t$ (`test_causal_04`).
3. **`committed_reservations`**:
   - Built exclusively for flights with $t < \text{scheduled\_arrival} \le t + H_{\text{lock}}$.
   - Estimated vacate time is derived strictly from scheduled times and minimum turnaround: `causal_est_vacate = max(f.scheduled_departure_min, plan_arr + f.min_turnaround_min) + f.buffer_min`.

## 2. Event Sequencing & Timestamp Tie-Breaking

### 2.1 Monotonic Chronological Sweep
Events in `evaluate_mode_b2_rolling_online_recourse()` are processed in strict arrival order:
```python
event_order = sorted(realized_flights, key=lambda f: (f.predicted_arrival_min, f.flight_index))
```
- Primary Sort Key: `predicted_arrival_min` (realized arrival minute).
- Secondary Sort Key: `flight_index` (unique integer index ensuring deterministic, stable tie-breaking for simultaneous arrivals).

### 2.2 Same-Timestamp Behavior ($t_1 == t_2$)
When multiple flights share the exact arrival timestamp:
1. Flight $i$ is processed at epoch $t$, assigned a gate (or unassigned if capacity exhausted), and docks (`locked_flights.add(f_i)`).
2. Flight $j > i$ is processed at epoch $t$. Its gate occupancy snapshot accurately reflects Flight $i$ as docked and occupied on its assigned gate.
3. Neither flight receives information about events occurring at $t' > t$.

## 3. Separation of Decision Policy and Ex-Post Evaluation

The execution timeline guarantees complete separation between online decision making and ex-post evaluation:

1. **Phase 1: Online Simulation ($t \in [0, T_{\max}]$):**
   - The rolling loop sweeps chronologically through all arrival events.
   - Assignments are committed into `active_assignments`.
   - Decision reasons and epochs are immutably logged into `decision_trace`.
2. **Phase 2: Simulation Termination:**
   - Solver execution timer stops: `runtime_ms = (time.perf_counter() - t0) * 1000.0`.
3. **Phase 3: Ex-Post Independent Verification:**
   - [`evaluate_gate_assignment()`](file:///D:/Study/Code/Python/Aelous/src/optimization/evaluation.py#L33-L100) and [`verify_hard_constraints_independently()`](file:///D:/Study/Code/Python/Aelous/src/optimization/domain.py#L984-L1006) audit `active_assignments` against actual realized flights and physical gate windows.
   - [`decompose_feasibility()`](file:///D:/Study/Code/Python/Aelous/src/simulation/monte_carlo_engine_v2.py#L140-L186) decomposes violations into physical conflicts, remote capacity violations, and unassigned flight counts.

### Verification Conclusion:
The information boundary $\mathcal{I}_t$ is mathematically and structurally intact. Future realization data cannot enter the decision policy either directly or via precomputed intermediate attributes.
